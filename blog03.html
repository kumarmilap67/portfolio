Typography & Readability

Choosing & blending between fonts that *feel* right — balancing elegance with clarity

Typography is more than just selecting fonts — it’s about creating an emotional voice for your content. Fonts communicate personality before a single word is read. The right pairing of typefaces can make your design feel calm, exciting, luxurious, or minimal.

1. Let the font express your tone

Cursive fonts like Dancing Script or Pacifico convey warmth, friendliness, and creativity. They are great for titles, headers, or quotes. But when used for long paragraphs, they can reduce readability — that’s where a balanced sans-serif like Poppins comes in.

  • Cursive (expressive): best for emotion, titles, and personality.
  • Sans-serif (neutral): ideal for clarity, readability, and modern layouts.
  • Pair wisely: combine a decorative font with a minimal one.
💡 Tip: Use cursive fonts for *impact*, not *information*.

2. Perfect pairing: cursive + modern sans-serif

Pair a cursive font for headings with a clean sans-serif for the body text. This contrast adds character without sacrificing readability. 

/* Example Font Pairing */
h1, h2, h3 {
  font-family: 'Dancing Script', cursive;
  color: #ffb6b9;
}

body {
  font-family: 'Poppins', sans-serif;
  color: #f8f8f8;
  background: #0d1117;
}

3. Readability rules to remember

  • Keep cursive fonts above 20px for clarity.
  • Use limited weights (400–700) to maintain style consistency.
  • Pair fonts with matching emotion — avoid mixing playful with corporate.
  • Maintain high contrast between text and background.

4. Font combinations that work beautifully

  • Dancing Script + Poppins → Artistic + Clean (used here!)
  • Pacifico + Open Sans → Friendly + Readable
  • Great Vibes + Lato → Elegant + Professional
  • Sacramento + Montserrat → Personal + Bold

5. A note on personality

Fonts set the *mood* of your brand. A flowing cursive can make your design feel personal and handcrafted, while a geometric sans-serif makes it feel futuristic and clean. The balance between emotion and structure is what defines your visual voice.
